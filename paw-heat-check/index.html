Quick paw safety tips

  • Do the 7-second test. Put the back of your hand on the footpath. If you can't hold it for 7 seconds, it's too hot for paws.
  • Walk early (before 8am) or late, after the ground has cooled. Footpaths stay hot for hours after sunset.
  • Stick to grass and shade. Avoid asphalt, metal covers and hot beach sand.
  • Flat-faced breeds, puppies, old dogs and dark-coated dogs overheat faster. Bring water.
  • Signs of burnt paws are limping, licking the feet, blisters or dark patches. See your vet.

Footpath temperatures are estimates from the weather forecast (air temperature, sunshine and wind) for asphalt in full sun. Shade, grass and concrete are cooler. Always do the hand test. This is general information, not veterinary advice.
